Read before writing any GTA script. Built 2026-11-18 from six transcripts the user chose (their words are not stored
here: this repository is public; only short quotes) plus each channel's upload numbers (`../research/reference-channels.md`).
We copy structure, pacing and technique. We never copy wording, jokes, persona, branding or opinions.

## Pace

Measured from transcript timestamps (spoken words over the opening stretch):

| Video | Words per minute |
|---|---|
| MrBossFTW news | ~160 |
| Broughy1322 | ~190 |
| Mount Chiliad theory | ~190 |
| DarkViperAU | ~225 (fast, dense, jokes land on the cut) |

**Target for the user's voice: 155-175 wpm** until their own pace is measured on the first recording, then their real
pace. A 12-minute news script is therefore about 1,900-2,100 words; a 15-minute lore script about 2,400-2,600.

## The hook (all six do the same three things in under 30 s)

1. **A bold, specific claim in the first sentence.** "You have never played Grand Theft Auto 5" (#2). "I've managed to
   find the inspiration behind everything relating to the Mount Chiliad mystery" (#6). "IGN just published its full
   interview... revealing a ton of additional details that weren't in the original coverage" (#1).
2. **Why this video is different or worth it.** #6: "there's a million videos on this mountain, so I looked at it a
   different way". #4: "For the last 13 years I've tested every single car in GTA 5". #3: "my over 15,000 hours".
   Authority in one line, never more. **Ours is sourcing and our own testing**, stated honestly ("every fact here is
   sourced on neonatlasguide.com", "we tested every one ourselves"). Never claim hours or expertise we do not have.
3. **A promise of what is coming, with its variety.** #2: some reveal cut content, some show shortcuts, "parts of it,
   frankly, are just disturbing".

Then **the first real item starts before 0:30** ("So, let's go ahead and dive into it" at 0:20 in #1; the first
cutscene at 0:30 in #2). No "hey guys, welcome back", no channel intro, no like-and-subscribe at the start. The one
exception that works is #5's 45 s cold open (a definition of "altruism" turned on its head), and only because the deep
dive's subject is an idea; keep that for a lore episode about a theme, not for news.

## The unit: claim, evidence, reaction

Every one of the six is a chain of short units. Each unit is 20-45 s:

- **Claim:** what we are about to show ("Robberies also don't appear to be restricted to specific stores").
- **Evidence:** the source, quoted or shown. News quotes the developer or outlet by name ("Rob Nelson says...",
  "IGN calls this..."). Lore shows the in-game proof: the email, the news report, the website, the radio, the file, the
  clip (#5 reads the cult's own manifesto and news reports; #3 plays the cut audio).
- **Reaction or punchline:** one or two sentences of the narrator's own view ("I love this", "I'm a little disappointed
  in that because...", #1) or a dry joke (#2's deadpan "he teleports, which honestly doesn't even seem that special as
  everyone can seemingly do it").

The reaction is what makes it a voice and not a reading, and it is what YouTube's reused-content rules look for. **Every
unit gets one honest opinion line written into the script** for the user to say in their own words.

Transitions carry the viewer from unit to unit: "Building off of that...", "Speaking of the police...", "And speaking on
how big the game is..." (#1); callbacks to earlier units (#2: "Based on everything you've seen... you would assume...
However").

## Structure by series

### `news` (MrBossFTW, #1): 10-15 min
- One news event, then **15-25 items**, biggest and most concrete first (robberies, then choices, then the world).
- A funny or surprising item around the middle to reset attention (#1: switching to Jason and finding him in the shower).
- Unknowns said out loud: "Who knows?", "we don't know exactly how... but". Never fill a gap with a guess presented as fact.
- Ending: one recap line, **the narrator's own favourite item** ("mine again has to be those random strangers"), then
  ask the viewer theirs. Keep the call to action under 20 s (#1's runs about 45 s, which is too long).
- Title: the formula in `../research/reference-channels.md` (opener + three concrete items + "& MORE!").

### `lore` investigation (DarkViperAU, #2): 12-20 min for us
- **A built-in order the viewer can follow:** #2 goes through the story mission by mission, so the viewer always knows
  where they are and that the end is coming. Pick an order (story order, map order, discovery order) and say it.
- Units of setup ("you remember...") > reveal ("but if we watch him...") > punchline.
- Chapters every 1.5-3 min with a specific name ("The Prologue Is Completely Fake Behind the Scenes").
- He admits a weak segment ("Consider this part the Scouting the Port of this video"): honesty keeps trust; do it rarely.
- His crude humour and persona are his. Ours: lighter, cleaner jokes the user is comfortable saying.

### `lore` facts series (DarkViperAU, #3): 12-20 min, numbered
- Opens with the series line and one authority line, then a clip, then fact one.
- **One fact per chapter, 20-60 s each:** claim > proof (clip, file, audio) > twist.
- **Vary the kind of fact:** hidden mechanic, cut content, lore, real-world story, glitch. One bigger story spanning
  2-3 chapters about two-thirds in (#3's real lawsuit, 13:25-15:13).
- Ending: thank the series viewers, point to the next episode or series (#3 announces his GTA 6 series): a loop.
- Ours: **"GTA 6 Facts You Don't Know #1, #2, ..."**

### `lore` deep dive (#5) and theory (#6): 15-30 min
- #5 follows **the order the player discovers things** (first encounter > email > news article > manifesto > camp >
  aftermath), quoting the game's own sources at length, then ends on an interpretation (what the satire is about) and
  a short personal note.
- #6 is a **list of matches** (game detail > the movie scene it came from > why it fits), then a conclusion that pulls
  every item into one theory. It argues against the other side by name ("A lot of people like to say this is a test
  ship... but honestly, if that were true...").
- Both hedge clearly: "it would seem", "I think we can assume", "very likely", "I actually believe". Theories are labelled
  as theories (`../CLAUDE.md` rule 3) and give evidence for and against.

### `cars` (Broughy1322, #4): 7-10 min
- Intro: credentials and curiosity in 30 s ("I've always had a curiosity about how the cars stacked up... so I tested
  every single one"), then **the method, explained in full** (track, laps, how top speed is read, how classes were
  decided). The method is what makes the numbers trusted; ours is written down before the first test (`../BRIEF.md`).
- **The data is published** (his spreadsheet and gtacars.net). Ours: a GTA 6 car-stats page on Neon Atlas, linked from
  every car video.
- A series announced up front ("one testing video for each class", "the next 13 weeks"), so every video promises the next.
- Countdowns run slowest to fastest; the winner is the payoff at the end.

## Shorts (from the same units)

A Short is one unit, or a top 5 made of five very short units: claim in the first second, proof on screen, one reaction
line, the payoff last. This-or-that: two cars or places, one stat per round, a verdict. Funny clips: the moment itself,
cut tight, one caption.

## Checklist before a script goes to recording

- [ ] First sentence is a bold, specific, true claim; the first item starts before 0:30.
- [ ] One authority line, and it is true (sourcing or our own testing).
- [ ] Every unit has claim, evidence (named source or in-game proof) and one reaction line.
- [ ] Series order is stated (biggest first, story order, discovery order, countdown).
- [ ] Unknowns and theories are labelled out loud.
- [ ] Ending: recap, the narrator's pick, a question; call to action under 20 s; the next episode named when it is a series.
- [ ] Words fit the target pace (155-175 wpm until the user's pace is measured).
- [ ] Read aloud once by the user; every stumble rewritten.
